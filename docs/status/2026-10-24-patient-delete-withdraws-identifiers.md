# Deleting a patient withdraws its identifiers (2026-10-24)

Date: 2026-10-24

**Implemented (no migration).** From the foundation recheck of 2026-10-19 ("a deleted patient's identifiers stay active"), re-read on `main` 528b158. `PostgresPatientsRepository.delete` only set `deleted_at`. The patient's identifiers stayed `active`, and the unique index `patient_identifiers_active_uq` (tenant, system, value digest) goes on holding the value: registering the same number for another patient answered "already registered to a patient of this organization" (and recorded a possible-duplicate flag) for a record nobody could open.

**The change.** The delete now runs, in the same transaction and after the patient update, `UPDATE patient_identifiers SET status = 'withdrawn', withdrawn_by = <the person deleting>, withdrawn_at = now()` for that patient's active identifiers. The repository's `delete` takes the actor (`principal.sub`, which the service already had), so the withdrawal names who did it, as a manual withdrawal does. An identifier withdrawn earlier keeps its earlier author. Because it is one transaction, a delete the soft-delete trigger refuses (live reports, merged records) withdraws nothing, and a withdrawal that fails leaves the patient not deleted. The development store follows the same rule (a deleted patient's identifiers are not treated as held).

**Tests, red first.** `patient-delete-identifiers.pg-spec.ts` (6, real PostgreSQL): active identifiers withdrawn by the deleter, an earlier withdrawal untouched; the same value can then be registered to another patient (refused before); another patient and another tenant keep theirs; an unknown or already-deleted patient withdraws nothing; a refused delete leaves them active; a failing withdrawal (synthetic trigger) leaves the patient not deleted. Five of six failed on the old code. Two deliberate breakages (withdrawal in a second transaction, withdrawal not limited to the patient) each turn a test red. `patient-identifiers.repository.spec.ts` covers the development store. Existing call sites in tests gained the actor argument.

**Limits.** The withdrawal is not a ledger record (identifier withdrawal never was). A deleted patient cannot be restored by the API, so there is no "un-withdraw". Possible-duplicate flags that named the patient are left as they are. Not verified in AWS (nothing here depends on it).
