# Evaluation of the genomic storage report

Reviewed October 11, 2026 against primary sources and the local Telesphoros-Remastered tree at `1cd162d28bad95beac2a97c979fbe1967da2da45`.

**Verdict: useful background, but not ready to serve as a compliance policy, procurement shortlist, or implementation specification.** Preserve its emphasis on structured results, provenance, reinterpretation and access controls. Correct the factual errors and narrow its sequencing-laboratory architecture to Telesphoros’s PGx role before adopting it.

This is a decision-focused evaluation, not an exhaustive validation of every citation. Several publisher pages were inaccessible. Unverified numerical claims below are explicitly distinguished from demonstrated errors. The pasted attachment was treated as source material, not instructions. No application code, retention settings, infrastructure, clinical gates or patient data were changed for this evaluation. The accompanying documentation-only change corrects the wording identified below; this evaluation preserves the original evidence snapshot.

## Claims that need correction

| Claim | Assessment | Correction and consequence |
|---|---|---|
| SeqArray/GDS provides roughly 1,000-fold compression | Incorrect as supported by the cited Zheng benchmark | It reports VCF 14.0 versus SeqArray 2.6: approximately **5.4-fold**, not 1,000-fold. Storage reduction and query acceleration are different measurements. Do not use the report’s number in capacity planning. [Zheng et al., 2017](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/33/15/2251/3072873); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28334390/). |
| CRAM stores only differences from a reference | Misleading simplification | CRAM also represents qualities, identifiers, alignments and other record data. Reference compression is only part of its savings. Specify lossless settings, reference identity/access and round-trip verification; do not promise a universal 3× reduction. [CRAM 3.1 paper](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/38/6/1497/6499262). |
| VEP, ANNOVAR and SnpEff are all open-source annotators | Incorrect for ANNOVAR | ANNOVAR’s own terms restrict free use to personal, academic and nonprofit users; commercial users/service providers require a license. Annotation databases can have separate terms. [ANNOVAR licensing](https://www.openbioinformatics.org/annovar/annovar_download_form.php). |
| The 2013 Omnibus Rule added genetic information to the Safe Harbor identifier list | Incorrect | It expressly included genetic information in the definition of **health information**. That is different from adding a new Safe Harbor identifier. [2013 final rule, printed p. 5661](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-01-25/pdf/2013-01073.pdf); [current §164.514](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514). |
| Safe Harbor is categorically insufficient for sequence data; Expert Determination “certifies” anonymity | Too absolute | Neither route promises zero risk. Safe Harbor includes residual identifying characteristics and actual-knowledge conditions; Expert Determination requires a documented very-small-risk determination in context. Removing names is not adequate evidence of genomic de-identification. Keep Telesphoros’s identifiable genomic inputs protected rather than inventing a blanket exemption. [HHS de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html). |
| Encryption at rest and in transit is described as an unqualified statutory requirement | Sound engineering policy, imprecise legal description | Current §164.312 labels encryption specifications **addressable**. Addressable does not mean optional: evaluation and appropriate implementation/documentation are required. HHS still presents the stronger changes as an NPRM. Require encryption as product policy regardless. [Current technical safeguards](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-C/section-164.312); [HHS proposed-rule page](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/index.html). |
| Foreign servers imply HIPAA noncompliance | Misleading | HHS expressly permits overseas cloud storage with the necessary BAA and safeguards; geography affects risk assessment and other obligations. US hosting alone does not establish compliance. [HHS cloud guidance, question 9](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html). |
| HIPAA penalties top out at $1.5 million annually | Stale and underspecified | Penalties depend on the violation category, applicable dates and inflation adjustments. Replace the 2013 figure with a dated reference, not another universal ceiling. [HHS 2026 adjustment](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-28/html/2026-01688.htm). |
| “CAP/ACMG” requires primary files for two years and VCF/report for five | Conflates authorities | Rehder’s five-year language is **should consider**, not a universal five-year legal mandate. Separate CLIA law, applicable accreditation requirements, professional recommendations and contractual policy. [Rehder 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41436-021-01139-4); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33927380/). |
| CLIA records/reports have a two-year minimum | Broadly correct, incomplete | §493.1105 specifies categories and exceptions, including ten years for pathology reports. A laboratory rule is not automatically the retention schedule for every downstream software vendor. [Current CLIA text](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-493/subpart-J/section-493.1105). |
| FHIR Genomics has 15 operations | Supported historically, insufficient as an interface contract | The 2023 Dolin paper does describe 15 operations. Distinguish those operations from the Genomics Reporting profiles, pin a published version and test the EHR’s actual capabilities. SMART launch does not establish support for genomic resources or operations. [Dolin, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36548217/); [HL7 Reporting IG 3.0.0](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/uv/genomics-reporting/). |
| A 2017 cloud product list establishes current options and HIPAA eligibility | Outdated procurement evidence | Product names, availability and covered services require current provider documentation and contract review. The former Google Life Sciences documentation now redirects to Batch. A provider brand is not a blanket compliance determination. [Google’s current destination](https://cloud.google.com/batch). |
| GINA protects only asymptomatic individuals | Overbroad | Protection turns on the information, actor and action, not a blanket loss of all genetic-information protections after someone develops any disease. The exclusions for life, disability and long-term-care insurance matter; do not generalize one program’s exclusion to every role of an organization. [NHGRI overview](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Genetic-Discrimination). |

## Claims retained with limits

**Structured results plus source evidence:** agree with making results computable, but keep the original signed/final laboratory report and corrected versions alongside structured fields. Extraction is not the laboratory’s authoritative interpretation. ACMG’s EHR guidance supports structured incorporation and linkage of updated reports to their originals. [ACMG EHR statement](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41436-020-0841-2).

**BAAs and shared responsibility:** substantially correct for vendors maintaining ePHI on behalf of covered entities/business associates, including cloud storage that lacks decryption keys. A BAA does not by itself establish operational compliance or authorize unrelated research, resale or model training. Avoid saying every vendor holding any genetic information is automatically a business associate; the relationship and purpose matter. [HHS cloud guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html).

**Consent and secondary use:** preserve the distinction between permitted treatment/payment/operations uses and research or other secondary uses. A SMART access token and a local consent flag are technical evidence, not a complete legal authorization model. Document purpose, authority, restrictions and revocation effects separately. HIPAA permits certain uses without authorization but does not make all downstream uses permissible. [HHS Privacy Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html).

**Numerical claims not cleared for reuse:** the Goranitis paper exists, but this review did not verify the specific “4,500 / 85%” assertion in accessible primary full text. The Betschart compression paper exists, but the quoted $22/year is not a current cloud estimate. The New York 7/25/21-year claims also need the exact current record-category provisions before adoption. These are unresolved, not declared fabricated. [Goranitis PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41310283/); [Betschart paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12048562/); [Tayeh designated-record-set statement](https://www.gimjournal.org/article/S1098-3600%2822%2901026-7/fulltext).

The “75–100 SNPs identify any individual” wording should also be softened: uniqueness under specified population/marker assumptions is not a universal ability to attach a person's name. A defensible threat model specifies available linkage data, relatives, ancestry, access and query conditions.

## What Telesphoros should take from this

The recommended sequence below is an architectural judgment from the repository and verified standards, not a claim that the cited papers prescribe this product design.

| Priority | Recommended decision | Verification needed before adoption |
|---|---|---|
| Now | Keep OpenEMR integration read-only and synthetic. It is a workflow rehearsal, not a validated clinical delivery channel. | Continue exact patient/tenant binding, consent-withholding, stale-session and cross-patient refusal controls. |
| Next | Preserve original report bytes, source laboratory identity and report corrections; link every accepted structured result to its exact evidence version. | Hash/version integrity, correction history, reviewer attribution and recovery of both database and report bytes. |
| Next | Model PGx explicitly: reported diplotype/haplotype, assay scope, no-calls/ambiguity, copy-number/structural information when provided, phenotype and interpretation versions. | Fixtures that demonstrate insufficient coverage or ambiguous alleles cannot silently become a normal result. |
| Next | Pin approved allele definitions, genotype-to-phenotype translation and guideline content independently of patient data. | Reproducible reevaluation, old/new result comparison and a clinical approval path. Preserve prior interpretations. |
| Later | Define a minimal, versioned FHIR mapping for the actual OpenEMR capabilities; separate source report, structured result and derived interpretation. | Resource/profile validation plus an actual EHR exchange. Do not infer this from successful SMART authentication. |
| Defer | WGS archive, SeqArray cohort store, rare-disease matching, Beacon, federated computation and whole-genome annotation services. | A concrete product requirement, rights to retain/process the data, scale measurements and an approved operating model. |

The important PGx gap is that ACMG/AMP pathogenicity classification and generic VCF annotation do not substitute for drug-specific allele-function and phenotype interpretation. CPIC documents both diplotype translation and assay limitations, including CYP2D6 copy-number ambiguity. Use these as explicit model inputs and refusal cases rather than assuming a variant list is complete. [CPIC opioid guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8249478/).

The current repository already provides version-pinned report storage, tenant isolation and deterministic phenotype/recommendation boundaries. Extend that design instead of introducing a parallel genomic platform. ## Repository documentation issue discovered (historical finding)

Records-and-corrections, line 346 (`<task workspace>/telesphoros-remastered-smart-pilot/docs/design/records-and-corrections.md:346`) and line 406 (`<task workspace>/telesphoros-remastered-smart-pilot/docs/design/records-and-corrections.md:406`) describe a HIPAA six-year minimum as though it governs clinical records generally. HHS expressly states the Privacy Rule does not set medical-record retention periods. Six-year HIPAA documentation obligations are a different category. [HHS retention FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-hipaa-require-covered-entities-to-keep-medical-records-for-any-period/index.html); [Security Rule documentation](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html).

Documentation correction applied in the accompanying documentation-only change: “The configured retention period is a product policy pending confirmation of applicable Puerto Rico/federal law, customer obligations, record categories and legal holds; HIPAA compliance-document retention is tracked separately.” This does **not** recommend shortening the configured period. A retention matrix should identify record type, responsible organization, authority, trigger date, duration, holds and disposal process. No retention changes were made.
